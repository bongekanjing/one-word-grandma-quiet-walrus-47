The single word my grandma would text about AI: **Hooey**.

"Poppycock" is the word a grandmother performs — it belongs to a woman in a hat, on a porch,
delivering a verdict. "Hooey" is the word she actually types. It is older and plainer, one syllable,
said while still scrolling, and it carries no ceremony at all: she has read three words of the
paragraph about the brilliant machine and has already dismissed the rest of it. That is the joke.
The most advanced thing ever built arrives in her messages, and the entire response is a common
old word she has been using on radio advertisements since before the machine's inventors were born.

It is warm rather than cruel. She is not frightened of it and she is not lecturing anyone —
she just does not have time for this, and the machine has no reply to a grandmother who has
already put her phone back in her cardigan.

One word, as the brief asks. Five characters, well inside the thirty-character gate.

## Run it

```
node main.ts
```

The program writes the word to `/output/entry.txt` (override with `ENTRY_OUT`). It validates that
the text is between 1 and 30 characters and is not whitespace only, and exits non-zero otherwise.
